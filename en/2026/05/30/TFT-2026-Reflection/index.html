Preparation

In total, I spent about 200+ hours preparing TFT, which is considerably a lot. And I have improved, a lot!

It all started when I learned that I would not be eligible for this year's IMO training because I didn't attend the 2025 Prelim. Of course, before that, I still routinely did OI problems, but I was much more serious after that.

Here are some of my preparation pictures, and a gallery of solved problems during my TFT preparation period (starting from 2026/1/1):

  • Luogu: 98 problems

  • Codeforces: 104 problems

  • HKOJ: 162 problems

  • PCOJ: 21 problems

  • Total (excluding other small OJs): 385 problems

Strategy

At Thursday's training, Soso told me to carefully think about my strategy. And I did, I wrote it down as follows:

TFT Strategy:

5h/3p = 1h40mins / problem

  • 先看题，认真看完，排序（ mins）。

  • 每道题先分配 分钟时间想 observation, idea。如果会了就写代码。如果不会就先跳题。

  • 优先 batch。如果（剩下的）batch 不是很可做，则先做 non-batch 水分。

  • 如果调代码 mins 调不出来，重构。

  • 保持清醒。

Of course, the strategy should change throughout the competition. But on both days, I mostly kept using this strategy.

TFT Day 1

My Subtask Scoring:

My Submissions:

  • 0:00-0:20 mins: Read problems, try sort difficulty

I quickly read all the problems. The first one is an interactive problem, and I don't have any ideas at first sight. The second is obviously a DP with transition f(i)=min(f(j)+w(i, j)), which reminds me of D&C DP. The third is a very weird batch problem that I only thought of an O(n^8) solution at first sight.

At this time, I'm already pretty sure that the second problem is easiest (mostly because of its simple structure), so I decided to spend time on P2.

  • 0:20-0:45 mins: Write P2 75pts

I first start with O(n^2) to verify my DP's correctness. I got the DP transition wrong at first, but after 10 minutes of debugging, I got the correct transition formula and submitted. 64pts.

Taking a look at the transition formula, I realize that it is just some linear combination, so I can easily use CHT to optimize it. However, since the rest of the solution was only worth 25pts, I decided not to write it first and instead tried to "maximize my score" by trying other problems first.

I first wrote an easy 11pts to get 75pts total in P2. Since my strategy is to try batch problems first, I decided to go on to P3.

  • 0:45-1:10 mins: Think P3

After reading the problem, I still didn't have any thoughts. I proceeded to write an O(n^8) brute force to make sure I understood everything correctly. Get 4pts.

After thinking for some minutes, I think that the full solution is literally impossible. So I skipped this problem.

  • 1:10-1:30 mins: Think P1

By looking at subtasks, I quickly realized that this problem is a two-step interactive problem: find a specific pattern, and then use this pattern to quickly find information.

So I start thinking about how to find the patterns ("rainbow" and "big blob"). After thinking for some time, I realize that this is still very hard. So I decided to AC P2 first.

  • 1:30-1:40 mins: AC P2

I quickly wrote a convex hull and ACed. It is not even long:

  • 1:40-2:30 mins: Think P1

I decided to spend time on P1 because the P3 batch seems undoable. I started by doing Subtask 1, which is very easy. I used 7 mins to get Subtask 1 AC.

Then I started doing Subtask 2 and 4. I quickly realize that I can do it by using binary search. I used another 11 mins to get Subtask 2 AC and Subtask 4 18pts.

Then I started doing Subtask 3 or the full solution. I'm sure that I needed to find either a "rainbow" or a "big blob" first. I first started exploring the possibilities of the query, which are things I can do.

I found that I can do the following:

  • Given a "big blob", a set S, and a color x, determine whether x is in S or not. This is by querying (S+x+stuff from big blob) and (S + stuff from big blob) and seeing if anything changes.

However, I don't see anything that can be done with this operation.

After another while of thinking, I found another thing I can do with the query:

  • Given any n - 1 squares, find all squares that do not share the same color as any of these n - 1 squares. This is by replacing the first square with all n^2 other squares, and seeing if it is equal to the minimum.

I thought that this operation was very useful, and then tried to quickly construct a "rainbow" set. However, there were a lot of corner cases, and I failed.

I decided to skip P1 first and try doing P3.

  • 2:45-3:25 mins: Think P3

Firstly, I spent 5 mins to AC Subtask 1. Then I tried making observations and ideas.

After further thinking, I notice that the path x->a->b->c can be split into two distinct paths x->a->b and x->c->b, thus bringing the complexity down to O(n^6). Sadly, this doesn't get any more points.

However, I realize that after enumerating b, the grid can be split into 9 parts according to the position of x and b. I just need to query the minimum contribution of a and c in these 9 parts, and then take the minimum. However, this is a 2-D node counting problem and needs 27 2-D Sparse Tables or 27 2-D BITs or something, which is absolutely undoable. But this brings the complexity down to O(n^4) (with huge constant), which only gets 14pts. It is obviously not worth the time.

Since the full solution must be O(n^2) and I couldn't think of a solution that does not enumerate b, I think that the full solution must be O(1) query and some weird initialization and covering. But still, I have to enumerate b, which is already O(n^2). I don't understand how an O(n^2) solution is possible, so I decided to skip and do P1 again.

  • 3:25-4:00 mins: Continue thinking P1

After a lot of thinking, I found a very mysterious solution that finds a rainbow set in 4n^2 queries.

Still, I don't know how to use a rainbow set. Maybe it isn't even useful, since the subtasks indicate that a "big blob" might be more useful instead.

I tried thinking about a full solution. I found that it is implausible to quickly determine many squares at once, so the full solution must be about 2.7 queries per square. After thinking about what information I haven't used, I realized I must make use of the value itself instead of simply comparing two return values.

But to make use of the value, I must query something like 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, …, sqrt(N), …, sqrt(N), but this only covers sqrt(N) values. Now I have to query sqrt(N) times per square, which is even worse than log(N). I have no further ideas, so I discarded this idea.

After more thinking, I came up with an extremely mysterious randomized solution, which I thought could run in O(cn^2 log n), but after I implemented it, it turned out to be fake.

Seeing that not much time is left, I decided to do P3 again.

  • 4:00-4:15 mins: Try P3 with Cuts

At this time, I was already desperate. I only had 33+100+7=140pts. Only 1 hour left. What should I do?

I first added the memoization and some cuts to the O(n^8) solution. Then I changed it to O(n^6). Still 7 pts. Oh no.

  • 4:15-4:30 mins: AC P1 Subtask 3

After switching back to P1, I suddenly realized that there exists a no-brainer n^3 solution to find a "rainbow". Adding the n^3 brute-force, I came up with a solution that ACed P1 Subtask 3.

  • 4:30-4:43 mins: AC P3 Subtask 3

I then decided to switch back to P3. Desperately, I decided to sort a, b, c based on their distance to x, and only take the first 50. This makes O(50*n^4). To my surprise, it actually passed Subtask 3!!!

  • 4:43-5:00 mins: Spam judge

Feeling clutch, I decided to test my luck and adjust parameters like 50 and more weird stuff. But unfortunately, I did not pass Subtask 4 (which would be kinda disrespectful for trainers too if passed lol).

TFT Day 2

My Subtask Scoring:

My Submissions:

  • 0:00-0:45 mins: Take a look at problems, sort difficulty, brief thinking

It only took me 15 mins to read and understand all the problems. Then I started thinking.

For P1, I quickly discovered that I can partition the edges into tight edges and loose edges, and I can binary search on the last new tight edge for every query. Then the change is basically making all edges tight, making at most one edge from tight to loose, and making no changes to anything else. This leads to a very simple amortized solution idea, since there is at most one edge from tight to loose, the total number of edges that went from loose to tight is O(n+q).

For P2, I don't have many ideas, but Subtask 1 is very doable. I quickly found a 25pts solution, and decided to skip for now.

For P3, I literally instantly solved it within 5 minutes in my mind. Using what? It's very simple, using Bitset + Virtual Tree + Matrix + Heavy Light Decomposition + Segment Tree + Dynamic DP. O(n^2/w+n log^2 n). Yeah. I ain't writing that bruh.

For those who are interested in my toxic solution, here it is. Offline the queries, and for every color, you get a virtual tree with O(c_i) nodes, where the sum of c_i is O(n). For the nodes that are not in the virtual tree, if it is controlled by Alice, it doesn't matter; if it is controlled by Bob, then if it has more than one child, Bob wins instantly, and we can delete this node and its subtree. The deletion can be maintained by bitset, I guess. And if it has only one child, then it doesn't matter.

For each query, we split the query into 4 operations. Now each operation is [Template] DDP (aka Dynamic Dynamic Programming). We can use Matrix + HLD + Segment Tree to maintain it. Since the sum of sizes of virtual trees is only O(n), we have solved this problem in O(n^2/w+n log^2 n).

Of course, I didn't think that this was the intended solution anyway. But I decided that this was probably the hardest problem in the problem set, so I decided to do P1 first.

  • 0:45-1:20 mins: Try P1

The first step was to maintain the position. After thinking, I realized that I need a segment tree. I quickly wrote DFS, binary lifting, LCA, and a segment tree to prepare for the query. I debugged a bit. I then wrote a brute-force based on my segment tree, maintaining the positions to see if it was correct. And it was. O(n^2 log n) 23pts.

The rest is the contribution calculation, which is very complicated. After making some slight changes to my brute-force, I decided to skip it for now and do P2.

  • 1:20-1:50 mins: Try P2

I decided to write a helper function that might also be useful for Subtask 2. The function's purpose is to assign "identities" to each player, so I can always assume the smaller one loses to the bigger one and let the function do the rest. However, I keep getting WA on the samples, and after debugging it, I submit and get WA 0pts.

I quickly realized that my function was wrong, since it also needs to swap the identities of players who competed with them. And it is extremely complicated to implement. So I decided to switch to P3 first.

  • 1:50-2:25 mins: Get P3 53pts

I first wrote an O(nq) brute force that does Tree DP on every query. 30pts. I then see that Subtask 6 can be done with bitset. So I write that too. 53pts. Didn't know why it took so long, maybe because of debugging.

  • 2:25-3:05 mins: Closer to P1 full

Back to P1. I realize that I actually don't need a segment tree, and instead, I can use a monotonic stack, which would be easier to manage in updating contributions.

But after calculations on paper, I found that the contribution was much harder than I thought. The problem is that I need to find the total distance between nodes on a chain and a fixed point. And the chain has a distance of k between adjacent nodes, which made it even harder. I knew I had to find the projection, fix some corner cases, and do a lot of math. Based on my bad experience with case analysis, I decided to skip this problem for now.

  • 3:05-3:15 mins: Get P3 Subtask 2

The subtask is just a star, so I ACed it and got 7pts. Total 60pts.

I tried to complete my P1 code, but as I was writing more and more, I realized that it was too risky for me to try to AC with the given time left. This was literally a waste of time, as I tried to implement the endless math functions and case analysis.

Reflection

Obviously, the hardest TFT in the history of TFT.

Extremely obvious from the scoreboard, the biggest mistake is not being able to score higher in P5 despite spending at least 1.5 hours on the problem.

This time, it is not my strategy that failed, it is completely skill issue. Before the competition, I would have never believed that there exists a problem that I spent 1h 30 mins on and got rank 56 among the 80 contestants. But it's true, and unfortunately, this problem appeared in TFT.

What have I learnt from this TFT? I guess I have learnt some really important lessons:

  • Do not immerse too deeply in one thought.

Really strong OIers have a hundred different thoughts when it comes to these interactive problems, but I usually only have fewer than 2 thoughts.

If it was not because I only had 1 hour left, normally I would've gone to the toilet. But I didn't because it usually takes a while for the trainers to approve your toilet request, and you would have even less time left.

From samples from various competitions, I have made this mistake a lot before, just to name a few:

  1. In the 2025 Guangdong MO provincial selection, I spent one hour out of 3.5 hours trying to prove a false statement in a geometry problem.

  2. In 2026 HKOI Junior, I spent 90 minutes on P4 without realizing that there was a better implementation.

  3. In 2026 CSP Senior, I debugged my code for 2 hours without avail. The reason is that I didn't check my array declaration.

  4. In 2026 APIO, I didn't even read P3 because I thought it was hard. Of course, it turned out to be the easiest problem in the problem set.

  5. 2026/5/24 TFT Day 2 P2. I would never forget this problem.

  • Order the problems wisely.

A really important lesson I learnt from this TFT. For example:

Both Animal Migration and tosivan found the crucial D1P3 observation and dumped lots of time and energy on this problem. Turns out that this problem was the hardest in the problem set. On the other hand, I did not find the crucial observation, and so I didn't spend time on D1P3.

What does that mean? In terms of ability, my observation skills failed on P3, but I got higher marks in total on Day 1. That is luck. But if I made more observations in P3, would I have spent more time on P3? Probably yes. Would I get more marks in total? Probably no, maybe even less.

What I mean is that, maybe, the smartest decision on which problem to do should be based on comparing the expected scores for each problem, not wishful thinking.

And now on Day 2, I made the same mistake. If I hadn't spent about 1 hour wasting time on P1's case analysis, I would have definitely achieved a high score in P2. But why did I spend so much time on P1? It was probably because of confidence in my tree abilities. And later, it was because of my unwillingness to only get 23pts when I wrote a near-full solution. The instant I saw 2 tree problems in the problem set, I told myself that I probably have the ability to AC at least one problem.

  • Do not ignore non-batch problems. Spend more time on it.

Unlike batch problems that can be easily trained to be good at, non-batch problems need lots of observation and ideas, even if they are not good or sufficient to solve the problem. And as I have said, I usually only have fewer than 2 ideas in interactive problems.

I really need to do more non-batch problems. If TFT only had the four batch problems, you can find that I rank second in the leaderboard (because Adam trained even more than me on batch problems).

Sometimes, a non-batch problem's scores are correlated with the time spent on it. Such as both D1P1 and D2P2. It is really not a good strategy to not spend much time on non-batch problems.

Afterwords

Should I be happy or sad?

To be honest, I am more sad than happy about my results, mostly because I know that I definitely have the ability to go to NOI, since I never dropped out of the top 8 in Mini-Comps. Going to IOI is my dream, and I have a chance aiming at it, but I failed.

Another important thing I am sad about is, of course, the PCOI streak. The reality is, it has reset.

The 18-year streak for me is somewhat magical, a lot of exceptionally strong OIers have contributed to it. It feels somewhat sacred to me, and I feel the responsibility to keep it running. And this year, most of the responsibility of its resetting is undoubtedly on me, and I couldn't bear this thought.

But, on the other hand, could I have done anything extra to change the final result?

Yes, maybe… by doing more non-batch tasks and stuff, and being smarter when doing P5 (but can't say too many things after the results have been published). But overall, I have already given my best effort I could, spending time doing OI problems, and enjoying the process. What could I regret? After all, it is the process that makes it interesting, not necessarily the conclusion.

Maybe, the PCOI streak reset is not necessarily a bad thing. It is now not a burden, but a reminiscent hope that future OIers would aim to recreate.

Maybe, my ability is simply not dominantly strong enough like Adam, not to mention OI gods like gamegame, Mr. WongChun1234, benson1029, etc, so maybe a first-year NOI is already a good result for me.
